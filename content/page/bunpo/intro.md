## Japanese Is SOV

English is **SVO**: subject → verb → object.

- *I eat an apple.* — Subject (I) + Verb (eat) + Object (an apple).

Japanese is **SOV**: subject → object → verb.

- <span lang="ja">わたしは りんごを たべます。</span> — Subject + Object + Verb.

The verb lands at the end. Every time. This feels backwards at first, but it's actually very consistent once you get used to it.

## Particles Are the Key

In English, word order tells you who does what. In Japanese, **particles** — small hiragana markers placed after each word — tell you the role of each word. That's why Japanese can move words around more freely: the particles keep the roles clear.

Think of particles as little labels: this word is the topic, this word is the object, this word is the destination.

## What This Section Covers

- **Particles 1:** は, が, を, に, で — the five you'll see most often.
- **Particles 2 (planned):** と, も, から, まで, より.
- **Sentence patterns (planned):** X は Y です, X が すき, X を ください.

## How to Study This Section

Grammar is not memorization — it's pattern recognition. Read each example sentence aloud, and notice *why* each particle is there. Don't try to translate word-by-word. Try to feel the structure.
